Accessibility for the medical care system

A guide dog for medical websites.

Pawthfinder is a browser extension that lives on patient portals. It reads what’s on the page and leads you through it — by voice, keyboard, or one small step at a time. It warns you about obstacles before you reach them.

It leads. You stay in control.

Tips for running the demo
  • Any username and password work on the mock portal.
  • Add &session=45 to a demo link to make the portal’s hidden timeout hit after 45 seconds. Watch Pawthfinder warn you, keep you signed in, or put your answers back.
  • Toggle “See this site without Pawthfinder” in the dog’s Stay close tab for an instant before-and-after.
  • Other goals to try: Refill my cholesterol medicine and Understand my test results.
  • Shortcuts: Alt+Shift+N goes to the current step, Alt+Shift+R repeats it.

The dog’s skills

🦮 Lead

Say your goal in your own words. The dog breaks it into steps, points at each next action, and re-plans if you wander off the path.

👃 Sniff ahead

Warns you about hidden time limits, robot checks, unreadable PDFs, unlabeled boxes and phone-only tasks before you get there — with a way around each one.

💬 Translate

Rewrites jargon, insurance terms and consent text at the reading level you pick. Reads aloud, summarizes, and explains each tricky word.

🎾 Fetch

Fills repeated forms from a profile you control. It always shows what it will fill first, never fills passwords, and never agrees to anything for you.

📣 Speak up

Drafts accommodation requests (an ASL interpreter, a quiet room, a longer visit, an exam table that lowers) and a printable accessibility card for the clinic.

🤝 Stay close

Voice, keyboard-only, large targets, high contrast, reduced motion, captions, dyslexia-friendly text. Switch freely. The dog uses chimes, barks, vibration or pointing to match.

💤 Rest stops

Take a break anytime. Your place and answers are saved on your device, so you can pick up where you left off, even if the portal signs you out.

Assist, don’t replace

A guide dog doesn’t take over. It leads, warns, and lets the person decide. Pawthfinder follows the same rules:

  • It points, you press. It only clicks when you ask (“press it for me”).
  • Your decisions stay yours. It explains consent and payment agreements but never ticks them for you.
  • No surprises. It shows everything it will fill before filling, and outlines what it changed.
  • Your health data stays with you. Profile, progress and saved answers live only in your browser. Passwords are never stored.

How it works

  1. Page understanding

    Reads the DOM and accessibility tree to find headings, fields and buttons by what they say, even on messy, non-semantic sites.

  2. Task planner

    Turns a goal into steps across several pages, checks which step you’re on after every change, and re-plans when a page isn’t what it expected.

  3. Audit engine

    Checks for missing labels, mouse-only controls, tiny targets, timeouts, PDFs and phone-only steps, then repairs what it can right on the page.

  4. Multimodal I/O

    Speech in and out, screen-reader announcements, keyboard shortcuts, sound and vibration cues, and a speech bubble that doubles as captions.

  5. Privacy by default

    Everything is stored on the device. No account and no server are needed for the core features.

In this hackathon demo, page understanding and plain-language rewriting use rules and a hand-made glossary, so the demo runs offline and gives the same result every time. The production design adds an LLM for unfamiliar portals and free-form rewriting, with health data removed or kept on the device.
